About the Study

LAN Sentinel: An AI-Based Network Intrusion Detection System for Local Area Networks Using Traffic Anomaly Analysis.

1.1

Background of the Study

Local area networks (LANs), especially Wi-Fi networks in schools, small offices and homes, are now the main way people reach shared resources such as file servers, printers, cloud services and IoT devices. The same wireless convenience widens the attack surface: anyone within radio range can attempt to join, and attackers who gain access can scan for hosts, intercept traffic, spoof addresses or move to other devices.

Existing defenses cover only part of the problem. Router firewalls apply fixed rules, and MAC filtering can be bypassed because MAC addresses can be cloned. Signature-based tools such as Snort and Suricata are effective against documented attacks but weak against new or modified ones, and they need regular rule tuning and technical expertise that most small networks do not have.

Anomaly-based detection builds a profile of normal behavior and flags significant deviations, so it can detect attacks without a known signature. Public datasets such as NSL-KDD, CICIDS2017 and UNSW-NB15 are commonly used for benchmarking, but they come from large or simulated enterprise networks and may not represent a small Wi-Fi LAN with a few dozen devices. Models trained on them can also produce many false positives in a different environment.

Two gaps motivate this study.
  1. Unauthorized devices and abnormal traffic in small LANs are rarely noticed because no tool continuously compares current activity with what is normal for that specific network.
  2. Many AI-based IDS studies stop at offline model evaluation and never deliver a working, real-time system that a non-expert administrator can use.
1.2

Statement of the Problem

Unauthorized devices and abnormal traffic in local area networks may remain undetected because common measures, such as Wi-Fi passwords, firewalls and MAC address filtering, mainly focus on prevention or predefined rules. Small and medium-sized LANs may also lack dedicated security personnel and affordable monitoring tools, which increases their exposure to data theft, service disruption and network compromise.

Specifically, the study seeks to answer:

1

How can the normal behavior of a LAN be profiled from passively captured traffic and device activity?

2

How effectively can a machine learning model separate abnormal traffic and unauthorized devices from that normal baseline?

3

How well does the system perform against simulated attacks and benchmark data, in terms of detection accuracy, false alarms and processing overhead?

2

Objectives of the Study

General objective

To develop an AI-based network intrusion detection system for local area networks that uses traffic anomaly analysis to detect unauthorized devices and abnormal network activity.

1
Capture & prepare data

Capture and prepare LAN traffic (normal and simulated attack traffic) by extracting flow-level and device-level features such as packet rates, packet sizes, protocol mix and MAC/IP activity.

2
Build the detector

Build an AI-based detection system that:

  • learns a baseline profile of normal traffic and known device behavior;
  • flags deviations as anomalies or unauthorized devices and shows them to the administrator on a dashboard.
3
Test & measure

Test against simulated attacks (port scanning, ARP spoofing and traffic flooding) and public datasets, using accuracy, precision, recall, F1-score, false-positive rate and processing overhead.

3

Significance of the Study

This study is expected to benefit:

Network administrators & IT staff

An automated way to monitor activity and be alerted to anomalies and unauthorized devices on a dashboard.

Schools & small organizations

A practical, lightweight monitoring approach whose processing overhead is measured in this study.

Network users

Better detection of unauthorized devices and abnormal traffic reduces exposure to data theft and service disruption.

Future developers

The system design and evaluation setup can be a starting point for work in other network environments.
