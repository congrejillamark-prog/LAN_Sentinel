Related Literature

What existing research and tools say about LAN security, anomaly detection and the Philippine setting.

Network security in LANs and the need for continuous monitoring

Passwords, firewalls and MAC filtering remain useful but do not continuously analyze network behavior. Scarfone and Mell (2007) explain that intrusion detection and prevention systems help identify suspicious activity, while the limits of rule-based detection make ongoing monitoring important, especially for small networks with few security staff.

Anomaly-based detection and traffic datasets

Chandola, Banerjee and Kumar (2009) surveyed anomaly detection, including its use for finding events that match no known pattern. Ring et al. (2019) stress realistic, well-labeled datasets. Kolias et al. (2016) introduced AWID for Wi-Fi research, Sharafaldin et al. (2018) presented CICIDS2017, and Ahmad et al. (2021) reviewed machine-learning and deep-learning approaches.

Lightweight machine learning methods

Isolation Forest (Liu, Ting & Zhou, 2008) isolates unusual observations without labels. Kitsune (Mirsky et al., 2018) uses an ensemble of autoencoders for online detection on low-cost devices, and LUCID (Doriguzzi-Corin et al., 2020) is a lightweight deep-learning DDoS detector. Sommer and Paxson (2010) caution that traffic varies across environments, so a model must be evaluated on traffic like the network it will protect.

Existing monitoring and intrusion detection tools

Snort (Roesch, 1999), Suricata and Zeek (Paxson, 1999) detect or log known threats but may miss unseen attacks. Fing, GlassWire, Nmap, ntopng, Wireshark and Kismet each cover a part of device discovery, scanning, traffic or wireless monitoring. Security Onion bundles several tools, and Darktrace uses self-learning machine learning. Their functions, complexity and intended users differ.

Network security challenges in the Philippine setting

Natonton (2018) found differences between public and private institutions in Butuan City in having a unit responsible for network security. De Ramos and Esponilla (2022) identified user education, cloud security, security strategy and unsecured personal devices as challenges for state universities and colleges, along with budget limits. Aquino and Noroña (2021) discussed the limits of reactive security tools and the potential of AI to learn normal network behavior, and Miranda, Tayag and Canlas (2025) highlighted cybersecurity skills needs among new graduates.

Synthesis

The research gap

The literature shows that LAN security needs more than basic access controls and predefined rules. Anomaly-based methods can identify deviations from normal behavior, datasets such as AWID and CICIDS2017 support model development, and Isolation Forest, Kitsune and LUCID show that machine-learning detection can be computationally efficient. At the same time, traffic differs between environments, so models must be evaluated carefully to manage false alarms.

The reviewed applications cover important but separate parts of monitoring: device discovery, packet inspection and signature-based detection. Broader platforms need more expertise or resources, and Philippine studies point to budget and staffing constraints in schools.

The gap: a practical, lightweight system that combines device visibility, machine-learning-based traffic anomaly detection and an administrator-facing dashboard for a small Wi-Fi LAN. LAN Sentinel is intended to fill it.
